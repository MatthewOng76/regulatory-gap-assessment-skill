Regulatory Gap Assessment Skill

A reusable workflow for line-by-line, section-by-section regulatory gap analysis in Excel — designed to preserve verbatim evidence, cumulative multi-document assessment, append-only reassessment history, and audit-friendly outputs.

View skill file View repository
What it solves

Regulatory analysis that stays rigorous

The skill forces verbatim regulatory evidence, verbatim internal evidence, controlled assessments, and explicit rationale instead of topical similarity or guessed compliance.

Why Excel-native matters

Built for sorting, filters, and pivots

Repeated rows are intentional. The design supports cumulative evidence across many internal policies and standards while remaining workable in a real Excel analysis workflow.

Why model-agnostic matters

Change the capsule. Keep the machine.

The model provides inference. The methodology, structure, context, and guardrails sit outside it — so the capability can move across frontier or local/private models.

How the skill works

  • Processes regulations sequentially, section by section
  • Splits compound wording into atomic requirements
  • Preserves verbatim regulatory wording before interpretation
  • Assesses internal evidence iteratively and cumulatively
  • Maintains append-only reassessment history
  • Keeps the workbook limited to Summary and Detailed_Analysis

Designed for real-world internal frameworks

  • One regulatory requirement may require 10–20 internal documents
  • Multiple internal standards may collectively satisfy one requirement
  • Internal consistency is tracked separately from regulatory compliance
  • Neighbouring standards are dependencies to investigate, not automatic gaps

What this is a practical example of

This skill is a concrete example of building capability outside the model layer. The workflow can use a frontier model when capability matters most, or a capable local/private model when privacy, deployment control, or data sensitivity matter more. The method does not need to change just because the inference layer changes.
